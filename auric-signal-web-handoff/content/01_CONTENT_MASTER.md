## 01 — Overview

### From market movement to an informed next step

Investment products are effective at showing prices, gains, losses, and allocation. They are less effective at connecting a change to three questions: Why did this happen? What does it mean for my portfolio? What should I evaluate next?

AURIC SIGNAL explores a signal-first decision journey. Instead of ending at a dashboard alert, the experience moves through explanation, supporting evidence, portfolio impact, scenario simulation, and a non-executing draft. The investor remains responsible for the final decision.

### What I designed

- Product strategy and experience principles
- Information architecture and core user flow
- Early direction and wireframe exploration
- High-fidelity mobile product interface
- AI Daily Brief and Signal Detail experiences
- Evidence and confidence presentation
- Portfolio Health and risk framing
- Scenario simulation and evaluation
- Rebalancing preview and saved-draft states
- Functional interactive concept prototype

## 02 — Context & Opportunity

### The interpretation gap

A portfolio dashboard can show that Bitcoin represents 58% of a sample portfolio, that technology-correlated exposure increased, or that the portfolio rose 2.24% in a day. Those facts do not automatically explain relevance, evidence quality, downside exposure, or the trade-offs of responding.

The opportunity was to design a coherent path between awareness and evaluation—without presenting a generated explanation as a recommendation or triggering a real transaction.

### How might we

How might we help a self-directed investor understand why a portfolio signal matters, examine the evidence behind it, compare possible responses, and preserve clear control over the outcome?

### Constraints

- Three-week independent concept scope
- Static sample data rather than live financial infrastructure
- No real trading or brokerage integration
- AI behavior represented as an interaction concept
- Financial language must remain neutral and non-prescriptive
- Complex information must remain legible on a mobile screen

## 03 — Research & Insights

### Exploratory foundation

The project used exploratory secondary research, competitive review, and product-pattern analysis rather than formal primary research. The resulting needs and principles are design hypotheses to be validated in future work.

### Observed product-pattern gaps

**Data without interpretation**  
Dashboards often prioritize totals and performance, leaving the user to connect a movement to personal exposure.

**Explanation without evidence**  
AI summaries can sound authoritative when sources, assumptions, and confidence boundaries are not visible.

**Action without comparison**  
A single suggested action hides opportunity cost, timing risk, and transaction friction.

### Design hypotheses

1. A prioritized portfolio signal can create a clearer entry point than an undifferentiated dashboard.
2. Evidence and portfolio context can help distinguish an explanation from a recommendation.
3. Scenario comparison can make trade-offs visible before a user saves a plan.
4. Explicit labels—estimate only, simulation only, and nothing executed—can reinforce user control.

## 04 — Product Strategy

### Product bet

If market movement, supporting evidence, personal impact, and scenario comparison are connected in one traceable journey, a self-directed investor may be better equipped to evaluate a change without surrendering control to automation.

### Experience principles

**Prioritize the signal**  
Surface the most relevant portfolio change before secondary market information.

**Explain the why**  
Connect movement to drivers, portfolio exposure, and the reason it matters.

**Show the evidence boundary**  
Separate observed sample data from generated interpretation and state confidence limits.

**Compare before acting**  
Expose alternatives, opportunity cost, timing risk, and estimated impact.

**Keep the user in control**  
Make every consequential step a preview or saved draft. Never imply execution.

### MVP concept scope

Included: Home, Portfolio Health, Insights, AI Daily Brief, Signal Detail, Evidence, Scenario Simulation, Scenario Evaluation, Rebalancing Preview, Assets, Watchlist, Profile, privacy controls, and saved-draft confirmation.

Excluded: account aggregation, live market feeds, real AI inference, brokerage integration, trade execution, tax optimization, automated rebalancing, and regulated financial advice.

## 05 — Design Development

### Four possible entry points

Early wireframes explored dashboard-first, insight-first, portfolio-first, and daily-brief-first directions.

**Dashboard-first** offered completeness, but risked giving every metric equal priority.  
**Insight-first** created explanation depth, but could separate the insight from the investor’s overall context.  
**Portfolio-first** anchored the experience in holdings and allocation, but required the user to locate the important change.  
**Daily-brief-first** created a clear narrative, but overemphasized a single content format.

### Converging on signal-first

The refined structure combines the strengths of those directions: portfolio context remains visible, but a priority signal establishes the next step. The journey then follows four stages:

1. **Understand** — see portfolio status and the priority signal.
2. **Explore** — review what changed, why it happened, evidence, and personal impact.
3. **Evaluate** — run a scenario and compare outcomes and trade-offs.
4. **Decide** — preview a possible allocation and save a non-executing draft.

### Key design decisions

- Make the priority signal more prominent than generic market content.
- Keep What Changed, Why It Happened, Drivers, Evidence, Impact, and Considerations in one traceable detail view.
- Treat confidence as a property of available evidence, not prediction certainty.
- Show calculation inputs and observed sample data inside the simulation explanation.
- Show current and suggested allocation together.
- Keep risk and trade-offs visible before the save action.
- Use persistent inline confirmation instead of implying that a trade occurred.

## 06 — System & Experience

### 1. Awareness

Home summarizes portfolio value, health, risk exposure, a priority concentration signal, and the AI Daily Brief. The hierarchy directs attention to the signal without removing access to the broader product system.

### 2. Explanation

Signal Detail connects the movement to a change in technology-correlated exposure. What Changed, Why It Happened, Drivers, Evidence, Portfolio Impact, and Considerations are presented as a continuous explanation rather than separate features.

### 3. Evidence

The concept distinguishes three evidence types: a price-correlation model, an allocation-drift tracker, and flow data. Strength labels communicate relative support inside the concept. They do not represent validated prediction accuracy.

### 4. Scenario evaluation

The investor can review no-change, trimming, and future-diversification paths, then inspect opportunity cost, transaction friction, and timing risk. The scenario remains a draft; balances do not change.

### 5. Simulation

A sample 15% Bitcoin decline is weighted by the portfolio’s current allocation. The interface shows estimated portfolio impact, changes to health and risk exposure, affected assets, updated allocation, and the assumptions behind the interpretation.

### 6. Rebalancing preview

Current and suggested allocations are compared before any save action. Estimated changes to portfolio health, concentration, and diversification are framed as previewed outcomes, not guarantees.

### 7. Save without execution

The final state confirms that the strategy was saved as a draft, is not financial advice, and did not execute anything.

## 07 — Trust & Validation

### Trust is designed through boundaries

The interface repeatedly distinguishes information, interpretation, simulation, and action:

- “Estimate only” and “Simulation only” labels
- Observed sample data separated from Auric interpretation
- Confidence described as evidence consistency
- Risk and trade-offs shown before saving
- “Nothing is executed” confirmation
- Privacy controls for personalization and analytics
- Demonstration-data disclosure
- No broker or exchange transmission

### Current validation status

The prototype demonstrates flow completeness, state transitions, information hierarchy, and the intended relationship between evidence and user control. No formal usability result is claimed.

### Future validation plan

Future research should evaluate whether participants can:

1. Identify why a priority signal matters.
2. Distinguish observed data from AI interpretation.
3. Understand that confidence is not prediction certainty.
4. Compare scenario trade-offs without reading them as recommendations.
5. Recognize that saving a strategy does not execute a trade.

## 08 — Outcome & Reflection

### Outcome

The project produced a complete mobile concept spanning portfolio awareness, explanation, evidence, simulation, scenario evaluation, rebalancing preview, privacy, and saved-draft confirmation. The final prototype demonstrates a signal-first architecture rather than a collection of disconnected finance screens.

### Reflection

The strongest part of the concept is not any single dashboard or AI summary; it is the traceable transition from change to evidence, impact, alternatives, and user-controlled next steps.

The primary limitation is validation. The three-week scope supported an end-to-end concept and functional prototype, but not primary research, model validation, live data, or regulated-product evaluation.

### Next steps

- Conduct moderated usability sessions focused on comprehension and trust boundaries.
- Validate terminology with users of different investing experience levels.
- Test whether confidence and evidence-strength labels are interpreted correctly.
- Audit accessibility with screen-reader and low-vision users.
- Define data provenance and model-governance requirements before any production implementation.
- Evaluate legal, compliance, privacy, and security requirements for a real financial product.
